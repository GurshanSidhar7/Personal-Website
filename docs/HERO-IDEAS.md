# Five functional home-page directions

The owner selected option 1, Follow the Work. It is implemented with Yamaha, Jonas, and LuckyLease examples. The other directions remain below as design history. All options use the existing paper, ink and vermilion visual system, keyboard-accessible controls, and reduced-motion support.

## 1. Follow the work — recommended

A clearer version of the earlier flowchart. Start with one real question, such as “How do you turn sales data into decisions?” A single readable path connects **the problem → my approach → the working result**. Switch between Yamaha analytics, Jonas AI workflows, or a software project; selecting a stage reveals a brief contribution and relevant tools. The final stage links to the actual experience or case study.

Unlike a diagram of abstract interests, this tells a concrete story on first view. Motion traces the selected path; every node has a job. Results stay within the verified work, with no invented metrics.

## 2. Career explorer

A compact interactive timeline. Selecting a company reveals the role, verified contribution, tools and dates. The visual thread connects experience over time. BMO is clearly marked incoming, with no claimed accomplishments. This gives recruiters a useful career summary without repeating the entire Experience page.

## 3. Mini data lab

A small table/chart with explicitly illustrative sample data. Visitors can remove a duplicate, handle missing values, or switch between raw and cleaned views and immediately see the effect. A short note explains the step and connects it to data pipeline and analytics work. This demonstrates a way of working through an understandable interaction, rather than listing tools. No company data or fictional achievements are used.

## 4. Project X-ray

An authentic project screenshot with a few selectable feature hotspots. On LuckyLease, selecting authentication, maps, chat or AI advice reveals the relevant technical flow and responsibility. The screen becomes a compact window into the engineering behind a real application, with a route to the full case study. It stays within one restrained canvas rather than another landing-page project list.

## 5. Search my work

A compact, keyboard-friendly search surface for questions such as “Have you worked with NLP?” or “What did you do at Yamaha?” Matching experience, projects and tools appear with short evidence and direct links. This is honest portfolio search over existing content, not a pretend AI chatbot. It gives visitors a fast way to find relevant work while still feeling personal and interactive.

## Owner refinement

The initial boxed implementation of option 1 was rejected as too large and repetitive. The current hero restores the earlier curved-line design: three connected work areas, one short selected summary, and a direct link. The large question/stage/tool panels have been removed.
